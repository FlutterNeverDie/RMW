# History Map World - Mongol Empire, Part 1
# 제목(안): How a Hunted Orphan Built the Largest Land Empire in History
# 형식: "# CH" = 장, "@" = 화면 지시(3~5초마다 하나 이상 바뀜), "N:" = 내레이터, "이름:" = 인물 대사(극화)
# 인물 대사는 극화. 실제 기록에서 온 말은 [기록] 표시. 숫자·연도는 학계 통설 기준, 논란 있는 숫자는 "maybe"·"chroniclers claimed"로 말함

# CH 0 | Hook | 0:00
@ black -> 1206 map of the steppe slams in, camera rushes toward Mongolia
N: In 1206, a man who had been hunted as a boy, captured, and left to starve stood in front of every tribe on the steppe.
@ portrait: Genghis Khan, title card "1206"
N: And they called him Genghis Khan.
@ borders timelapse 1206 -> 1279, red spreading fast, counter ticking
N: Seventy years later, his family ruled from the Pacific to the Black Sea. The largest land empire in history.
@ split screen: tiny Mongol population icon vs huge China / Persia / Rus
N: There were maybe a million Mongols. Their enemies had tens of millions.
N: So how did a nation of herders do it?
@ episode title card: "THE MONGOL EMPIRE - PART 1: THE OUTCAST"

# CH 1 | The Outcast | ~0:40
@ map: Onon river, northern Mongolia, 1160s; pin "Temüjin born, c. 1162"
N: His name was Temüjin. His father, Yesügei, was a chief. Not a big one, but a chief.
@ portrait: Yesügei, then a Tatar camp, cups
N: When Temüjin was about nine, his father rode home past a Tatar camp, shared their food, and was dead within days. Poisoned.
@ the clan's tents ride away; one tent left alone on an empty map
N: And then the clan did something brutal. They packed up and left. The widow, Hoelun, and her children were abandoned on the open steppe to die.
@ close-up: Hoelun digging roots, children fishing
N: They survived on roots, wild onions and fish. Temüjin learned early what the steppe really was: no one protects you. You protect yourself.
@ portrait: Taichiud chief; Temüjin in a wooden cangue
N: A rival clan, the Taichiud, hunted him down and locked his neck in a wooden collar.
TAICHIUD CHIEF: The son of Yesügei. Keep him alive. For now.
@ night, river, Temüjin hiding in the water; a guard looks right at him and walks away
N: One night he escaped and hid in a river. A guard spotted him... and said nothing. That man's family would later be rewarded for the rest of their lives.
@ wedding: Temüjin and Börte; then Merkit raiders sweep in, arrows on the map
N: He grew up, married a girl named Börte, and almost immediately lost her. Merkit raiders kidnapped her.
@ portrait pair: Toghrul (Ong Khan) and Jamukha; three arrows converge on the Merkit camp
N: Temüjin had no army. So he did what he would do for the rest of his life: he found allies. His father's old blood brother, Toghrul, and his childhood friend, Jamukha.
TEMÜJIN: Lend me your riders. I will pay you back. In everything.
@ Merkit camp collapses, Börte rescued
N: Together they crushed the Merkits and got Börte back. The outcast boy was suddenly someone men wanted to follow.

# CH 2 | Uniting the Steppe | ~2:10
@ map of the steppe split into tribes: Tatars, Keraites, Naimans, Merkits, Jamukha's followers
N: The steppe at the time was a mess of tribes raiding each other forever. Tatars, Keraites, Naimans, Merkits.
@ Temüjin and Jamukha side by side, then a crack runs between them
N: Temüjin and Jamukha led together for a while. But the two friends wanted different things.
JAMUKHA: The old families rule. That is the way of the steppe.
TEMÜJIN: I don't care who your father was. I care if you're loyal and if you can fight.
@ battle arrows: Dalan Baljut, Temüjin's arrow pushed back
N: Around 1187, they went to war. Temüjin lost. Badly.
@ black flash; text "Chroniclers say..."
N: The chronicles claim Jamukha boiled seventy prisoners alive. Whether it's true or not, it backfired. Men started leaving Jamukha... and joining Temüjin.
@ 1201 battle, an arrow hits Temüjin's horse; enemy archer portrait
N: In one battle an enemy archer shot Temüjin's horse out from under him. After the fight, the archer was captured, and he admitted it.
ARCHER: It was me. Kill me if you want. Or let me fight for you.
TEMÜJIN: A man who tells the truth when it can get him killed... From now on your name is Jebe. "The Arrow."
@ portrait: Jebe; portrait: Subutai, "son of a blacksmith"
N: Jebe would become one of the greatest generals who ever lived. Next to him rose another nobody: Subutai, the son of a blacksmith. Remember these two names.
@ quick cuts, one per tribe, each turning the same color: 1202 Tatars, 1203 Keraites, 1204 Naimans
N: Then Temüjin went on a run. 1202, the Tatars, the people who poisoned his father. 1203, the Keraites, even his old ally Toghrul. 1204, the Naimans.
@ Jamukha alone, his last men tie him up and bring him in
N: Jamukha's own men finally handed him over. Temüjin executed the traitors who betrayed their lord... and then his old friend too.
@ 1206 great assembly on the map, banners, the whole steppe in one color
N: In 1206, at a great assembly by the Onon River, every tribe on the steppe swore to one man. Temüjin took a new name: Genghis Khan.

# CH 3 | The Machine | ~4:10
@ the army appears as dots: 10, then 100, then 1,000, then 10,000
N: Now here's the real secret. Genghis didn't just win the tribes. He broke them.
N: He split everyone into units of ten, a hundred, a thousand and ten thousand, and mixed the tribes together on purpose.
@ two soldiers from rival tribes, same uniform, same unit
N: Your old tribe didn't matter anymore. Your unit did. If one man ran, the whole ten could be punished.
@ portraits: Jebe, Subutai, Muqali; labels "archer's prisoner", "blacksmith's son", "slave's son"
N: And officers were picked by talent, not birth. A prisoner, a blacksmith's son... in Genghis's army they could command ten thousand men.
@ horse archer animation: five horses per rider, arrows, feigned retreat arrows on the map
N: Every man was a horse archer from childhood. Each rider had several horses, so they could ride for days. And their favorite trick? Fake a retreat, pull the enemy apart, then turn around.

# CH 4 | Breaking the Great Wall | ~5:00
@ zoom out: the Mongols vs the Jin dynasty in north China, population counter "Jin: tens of millions"
N: Genghis now had a war machine. And to the south sat the richest target on Earth: China. The north was ruled by the Jin dynasty, and the Jin had humiliated the Mongols for generations.
@ quick: Western Xia submits, 1209-1210
N: First he knocked out the Western Xia kingdom, so they couldn't stab him in the back.
@ 1211 arrows through the mountain pass: Yehuling, "Badger's Mouth"
N: Then, in 1211, at a mountain pass called Badger's Mouth, the Mongols met a Jin army many times their size... and destroyed it.
@ siege of Zhongdu (modern Beijing), 1215, city fires
N: By 1215, they had taken the Jin capital, Zhongdu. Today we call it Beijing.
@ portrait: Muqali left in China; Genghis turns west, map pans across Central Asia
N: Genghis left his general Muqali to keep grinding down the Jin. Because something in the west had caught his attention.

# CH 5 | The Caravan | ~5:50
@ map: Khwarazmian Empire, huge, from Iran to Afghanistan to Central Asia
N: West of the Mongols lay the Khwarazmian Empire. Rich. Huge. Its army may have outnumbered the Mongols several times over.
@ portrait: Shah Muhammad II
N: Genghis actually wanted trade with them. So he sent a caravan, hundreds of merchants loaded with goods.
@ caravan icon walking on the map to Otrar
N: At the border city of Otrar, the governor, Inalchuq, accused them of being spies... and had them killed.
@ envoy scene: three envoys, one beheaded, two with shaved beards walking home
N: Genghis gave the Shah one more chance. He sent envoys to ask for justice. The Shah executed one and sent the others back humiliated.
@ Genghis on a mountain at night, alone
N: The chronicles say Genghis climbed a mountain and prayed for three days.
GENGHIS: I did not start this. Give me the strength to finish it.
@ black. Text: "1219"

# CH 6 | The Destruction of Khwarazm | ~6:40
@ 1219 map: Mongol arrows split into several armies heading for different cities
N: In 1219, the Mongols came. Not as one army, but several at once, from different directions.
@ Otrar siege; Inalchuq portrait; text "legend"
N: Otrar fell after months. Legend says Inalchuq was executed by having molten silver poured into his eyes and ears.
@ arrow across the Kyzylkum desert to Bukhara
N: Meanwhile Genghis himself crossed a desert the Shah thought no army could cross, and appeared behind his lines at Bukhara.
@ Bukhara mosque, Genghis speaking to a crowd [기록]
N: According to the Persian historian Juvaini, Genghis told the people of Bukhara:
GENGHIS: [기록] I am the punishment of God. If you had not committed great sins, God would not have sent a punishment like me upon you.
@ Samarkand, 1220, walls fall
N: Samarkand, the jewel of the Silk Road, fell next.
@ Shah Muhammad fleeing west on the map, two arrows chasing: Jebe and Subutai
N: And the Shah? He ran. Genghis sent Jebe and Subutai after him with orders not to come back until he was dead.
@ Caspian Sea, tiny island, Shah alone
N: They chased him all the way to the Caspian Sea, where he died on a small island, sick and alone.
@ quick cuts with counters: Urgench (flooded), Nishapur, Merv
N: The cities that resisted paid a horrible price. At Urgench, the Mongols broke the dams and flooded the city. At Nishapur, where Genghis's son-in-law had been killed, chroniclers say not even the cats and dogs were spared.
@ text: "chroniclers claimed 700,000+ dead at Merv" with a question mark
N: For Merv, chroniclers claimed hundreds of thousands of dead. Historians think those numbers are exaggerated. But even the exaggeration tells you how terrified the world was.
@ 1221 Indus river: Jalal al-Din's army against the river, Mongols closing in
N: The Shah's son, Jalal al-Din, made one last stand at the Indus River in 1221. He lost. And then he rode his horse off a cliff into the river and swam away.
@ Genghis on the riverbank, watching [기록]
N: Genghis stopped his archers from shooting, and turned to his sons:
GENGHIS: [기록] Such a son should a father have.

# CH 7 | Cliffhanger | ~9:10
@ the map: Khwarazm now Mongol color, empire 1221 vs 1206 side by side
N: In fifteen years, the outcast boy had gone from one abandoned tent to an empire stretching from Beijing to Persia.
@ Jebe and Subutai's arrow, still moving west around the Caspian, toward the Caucasus
N: But Jebe and Subutai never came home. With maybe twenty thousand men, they kept riding west. Around the Caspian Sea. Over the Caucasus mountains.
@ Europe appears at the edge of the map, Rus principalities lit up
N: Straight toward a world that had no idea they existed.
@ end card: "PART 2: THE FIRST CLASH WITH EUROPE", map of the Kalka River
N: Next time: the first time Europeans ever met the Mongols. It did not go well for Europe.
